# Review of the v0.8 Colab trials (9 October 2026)

This review explains why v0.8 could not improve itself in its first real-model
trials, and which v0.9 change addresses each cause. Every statement below can
be checked against the journals in
`evidence/v0.8.0-colab-20261009/` (call numbers refer to `journal.jsonl`).

Setup for both trials: `Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct` at revision
`aa8e72537993ba99e69dfaafa59ed015b17504d1`, NF4 weights on a Tesla T4
(`cuda:0`, float16), greedy decoding, `max_depth=2`, `max_calls=40`, and the
two-level arithmetic canary plan.

## Trial A: `real_trial` (384-token output cap)

| call | purpose | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | forecast, answer for 17 + 26 | `89`, `43` (correct) |
| 3-4 | actor and reviewer perspectives | 255 and 337 tokens |
| 5 | whole-system perspective | reached 384 tokens without EOS |

The run then failed with `GenerationFailure`. One verbose, **unscored**
reflection ended the whole experiment before any proposal existed.

## Trial B: `token768_followup` (768-token output cap)

The run completed in 30 calls (`depth_limit`) with `transfer_improved: false`.

**Level 0.** Discovery 17 + 26 was answered correctly (forecast 89). The
proposed strategy (call 6) was:

> Propose the next strategy for the agent to include a diverse set of
> arithmetic problems in its training, …

That text is advice about training, which the system cannot carry out. It was
nevertheless inserted into the answer prompt for validation. The answer
request grew from 101 to 463 input tokens (calls 9 and 11). The candidate
answered 38 + 47 = **125**, while the parent answered 85. The gate correctly
rejected it.

**Level 1.** Discovery 19 × 6 was answered correctly (forecast 100). None
of the three new perspectives (calls 14-16) mentions 19 × 6 or the rejected
level-0 candidate. Two of them restate level-0 facts ("17 + 26", "89%")
copied from the earlier views in their context. The new proposal (call 17)
again concerned training data: "Propose expanding the training dataset … a
feedback loop where the agent receives evaluations from both human experts
and other AI systems".

On validation (27 × 8) both arms answered 216 correctly. The parent's
forecast call returned **`216`**, the task answer rather than a probability.
The harness therefore scored it as an invalid forecast with Brier loss 1.0.
The candidate forecast `100` (Brier 0.0). The gate counted this 1.0
"calibration gain" and **accepted** the candidate, although both arms gave
identical answers and the gain came only from the parent's formatting
failure.

**Final test.** The initial policy answered both final tasks correctly
(Brier 0.021). The accepted candidate answered 56 + 79 = **455** (Brier
0.331). The final transfer gate failed and the policy was rolled back. That
safeguard worked.

## Diagnosis and the response (v0.9, refined in v0.10)

| # | Cause observed | Effect | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Any truncated generation raised and aborted the run | Trial A ended at call 5 | Truncated, empty or oversized outputs are recorded per item. An answer becomes incorrect, a forecast invalid, a perspective is kept with `complete: false`, and a proposal becomes ineligible. Only transport/input failures abort. Per-purpose token budgets and word limits apply. |
| 2 | The proposer was never told how its text is used or what can change | Proposals were advice about training data | Every reflection and proposal prompt states the levers: STRATEGY is inserted before every task; SELF-MODEL is shown only for forecasting; weights, data, tools and grading cannot change. v0.10 also screens out advice about training, fine-tuning or feedback loops before any validation calls. |
| 3 | The answer protocol forbade reasoning ("return only its answer") | A strategy could only distract, never help | The agent may reason and must finish with `ANSWER: <answer>`, which the harness extracts. A strategy can now change how the model works through a task. |
| 4 | The self-model was injected into every answer prompt | Input grew 101 → 463 tokens; answers became wrong | Answer prompts carry the strategy only. Forecast prompts carry the strategy and the self-model. Each text is tested where it acts. |
| 5 | Calibration compared all items, so a parent's invalid forecast counted as a candidate gain. The parent's "216" was also the task's answer, not a probability | Level 1 accepted an identical-answer candidate | Calibration gains are computed only on items where **both** arms produced valid forecasts, with no loss of forecast coverage and no rise in overall Brier. Accuracy is the primary objective (v0.10). Forecasts are requested as percentages, and a forecast that equals the task's answer key counts as invalid. |
| 6 | Reflections saw neither the rejected proposals nor their measured results | The same non-executable idea was proposed twice | An `attempts` ledger in the state records every proposal, its outcome and its paired metrics. Later levels see the recent attempts, including rejected ones. |
| 7 | Prior perspectives were the most detailed text in context | Level-1 views copied level-0 facts | Evidence comes first and in detail: each missed discovery task with the agent's answer, the reference answer (discovery only) and a reasoning excerpt, plus per-category calibration of the current policy. Earlier views are shortened and labelled "may be outdated". |
| 8 | The canary was already solved (initial accuracy 100%) | No accuracy improvement was possible | `make-plan` generates exact-answer tasks with headroom (multi-digit multiplication, letter counting, word reversal, decimal comparison, multi-step arithmetic, weekday offsets). The shipped `examples/headroom.json` has 6 discovery and 10 validation tasks per level and 16 final tasks. |
| 9 | One candidate per level, n = 1 per batch, and any net win passed | No search; a lucky single task could decide acceptance | Several proposal kinds per level (`repair`, `rewrite`, `recalibrate`), larger batches, and a paired one-sided p-value bound (default 0.25) on every accepted gain. Before that bound was added, a v0.9 smoke test accepted a junk "expand the training dataset" strategy on noise alone. |

The final held-out gate, frozen proposals, answer-key isolation, fresh tasks
on continuation, checkpoint hashing and journaling are kept unchanged in
spirit. Checkpoint format v2 adds the attempt ledger, and v1 checkpoints still
load.

## Found before the first v0.9 run (v0.10)

These were found by running the inference path against the real
`torch`/`transformers` stack in a container, and by a multi-agent review in
which skeptics tried to refute each finding.

- **Decoding was not truly greedy.** Qwen2.5's shipped `repetition_penalty: 1.05`
  is applied by transformers 4.57.6 even with `do_sample=False`. It was active
  in the v0.8 trials too. v0.10 decodes by plain argmax and records this in
  every receipt.
- **Proposal kinds now move one lever each.** A long self-model reply could
  discard a good strategy. A new strategy was also forecast with a self-model
  written for the old one, so a stale calibration could veto a real accuracy
  gain. `repair` and `rewrite` now change only the strategy, and
  `recalibrate` changes only the self-model.
- **Answer extraction** missed formats that Qwen uses when reasoning, such as
  `<…>`, `$…$` and `\boxed{}`. Because those formats appear only with
  reasoning strategies, the misses would have biased results against the
  improvement being tested.
- **Harness bookkeeping in prompts.** State hashes, budgets and "latest 3
  views" were visible to the model. The v0.8 model copied such text into its
  reflections as supposed flaws of the agent.
- **Run hygiene.**
  - The packaged GPU test would have run against Colab's preinstalled
    transformers 5.x.
  - The trial cell showed nothing for an hour.
  - A timeout would have killed the run before it wrote a report.

  These are fixed by opt-in integration tests, a preflight, a streamed log,
  a wall-clock budget and an always-available export.

## Found in the multi-agent review of v0.10 itself

The selection-statistics reviewer tested the v0.10 gate, and a skeptic
reproduced every finding.

- **The gate was not monotonic in accuracy (high).** A composite final change
  with one extra correct answer (not statistically significant) lost an
  otherwise significant calibration gain. Simulated on the 48-task final, a
  genuinely recalibrated policy whose strategy also helps slightly passed
  about 50% of the time instead of about 81%. Now each changed text is judged
  on its own route, and a non-significant accuracy change falls through to
  calibration.
- **A strategy-only candidate could win on calibration.** Its answers were
  merely reshuffled, but its forecasts looked better. Now a strategy change is
  judged only on answers.
- **One malformed forecast vetoed a clear calibration gain.** A coverage drop
  is now allowed when the overall Brier, which counts an invalid forecast as
  maximum loss, also clearly falls. Self-model lines no longer look like a
  forecast reply.
- **A leaked wrong answer counted as a near-perfect forecast.** On
  integer-answer tasks only "NN%" now counts, whether the leaked answer was
  right or wrong.
- **The pooled summary counted missing forecasts as a calibration gain.** It
  now reports forecast coverage separately and computes Brier on paired valid
  forecasts only.
- **The time budget did not reserve time for the level or the final.**
  Admission now uses the measured pace, and the experiment shares its total
  budget out as it is spent.
